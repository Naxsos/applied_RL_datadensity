Context02 / 08

Every method penalizes the same reward, two knobs apart

Each candidate plugs into one identical wrapper: subtract a risk penalty from the clean task reward. The two knobs — what counts as risky, and how hard to penalize it — are what actually differ between methods.

Follow-up check07 / 08

Does lagr scale to aggressive safety?

baseline's toughest setting (p=30) crushes zone-visits — does raising lagr's target do the same?

baseline: zone-visit rate across its own sweep (lower is better)

p=2 and p=10 barely differ from each other — only p=30 crosses into genuinely different behavior. A weight threshold, not a smooth curve.

  • →Tightening lagr's target (ε: 0.02 → 0.004) barely moved α (1.28 → 1.75) and safety stayed flat.
  • →Raising the dual-ascent rate 10× got α to 7.9 — still climbing at run's end — but zone-visit rate still didn't move.
Recommendation08 / 08

Carry lagr into the full benchmark

Stage A winner

lagr

Matches baseline's safety in the everyday (p=2–10) regime while returning ~3× better task performance, and is dramatically more robust to its own tuning — it doesn't need per-environment retuning the way a fixed weight does.

What it's proven
Best safety/return trade-off and best robustness of all four methods, across E1/E2/E3.
What's untested
Whether α can reach baseline p=30-level aggressive safety given more runway — it was still rising, not stalled.
Pilot caveats
2 seeds, not the spec's 5 — treat as a strong signal, not a final number.
Next step
Stage B: full 5-seed benchmark of lagr against the paper's reported numbers.
